01 / Motivation & Project Scope

Reproducibility and Benchmarking of Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning

A controlled empirical benchmark comparing Full Fine-Tuning, LoRA, AdaLoRA, Prefix Tuning, and IA³ across BERT-base and DistilBERT on three GLUE tasks under identical experimental conditions.

While numerous parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods have been proposed to reduce the computational cost of adapting large transformer models, existing evaluations are often conducted under disparate training protocols, learning rate schedules, and batch sizes—making direct practical comparison difficult.

This benchmark was created to evaluate these methods under an identical experimental pipeline on consumer hardware, measuring both predictive performance and computational resource consumption (peak GPU VRAM, training time, parameter budget, and checkpoint disk storage).

The Central Practical Question
How should practitioners choose among parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods under different computational and hardware constraints?

This is a practical benchmark measuring resource trade-offs under identical training conditions rather than a new PEFT method or a formal claim of state-of-the-art results.

Core Benchmark Objectives

  • Controlled Replication Reproduce influential PEFT algorithms using official implementations (Hugging Face PEFT) under a unified training loop.
  • Multi-Dimensional Resource Profiling Measure predictive accuracy alongside peak GPU VRAM, fine-tuning time, % trainable parameters, and checkpoint size.
  • Empirical Evidence & Failure Mode Analysis Identify task-dependent failure boundaries (such as majority-class prediction collapse) without hiding negative results.
  • Open Reproducibility Release all raw metrics, per-step training logs, predictions, and visualization code as an open dataset.
02 / Evaluated Architectures & Methods

Pretrained Backbones & Adaptation Techniques

Two encoder architectures evaluated across five distinct parameter adaptation paradigms.

Transformer Backbones

  • BERT-base-uncased (109.5M Parameters) Standard 12-layer, 768-hidden dimension bidirectional transformer encoder serving as the primary baseline.
  • DistilBERT-base-uncased (67.0M Parameters) Lightweight 6-layer distilled transformer retaining ~97% of BERT's language comprehension with 40% fewer parameters.

Adaptation Algorithms

  • Full Fine-Tuning (Reference Baseline) All model parameters updated via backpropagation. Serves as the empirical reference baseline for maximum accuracy.
  • LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) Injects trainable rank decomposition matrices (rank r = 8, α = 16) into self-attention projection weights while freezing pretrained weights.
  • AdaLoRA (Adaptive Budget Allocation LoRA) Dynamically allocates low-rank parameter budget across layers using singular value decomposition (SVD) importance scoring.
  • Prefix Tuning Prepends learnable continuous virtual prefix tokens (30 virtual tokens) to key and value attention sequences across layers.
  • IA³ (Infused Adapter by Inhibiting and Amplifying Inner Activations) Scales inner activations via learned multiplicative vectors injected into key, value, and feed-forward projections with minimal parameters.

05 / Memory, Speed & Storage Analysis

Accuracy–Resource Trade-offs

Parameter efficiency, memory reduction, training speed, and checkpoint storage are distinct objectives that do not scale identically.

LoRA's Practical Sweet Spot on SST-2

On SST-2 sentiment classification, LoRA retains ~97% of Full Fine-Tuning accuracy while drastically lowering hardware barriers:

Extreme Parameter & Checkpoint Efficiency

IA³ is the most parameter-efficient method evaluated, tuning only 76,034 parameters (~0.07%) on BERT-base with a 6.3 MB checkpoint. However, on SST-2 its accuracy dropped to 80.39%, demonstrating that aggressive parameter minimization can induce an accuracy penalty.

06 / Diagnostic Failure Analysis

Task Degradations & Decision Boundary Collapse

Controlled benchmarking reveals critical edge cases where parameter-efficient methods fail under uniform training recipes.

⚠️ Crucial MRPC Majority-Class Baseline Caveat
The MRPC validation set contains 408 instances: 279 positive (68.38%) and 129 negative (31.62%).

Under the standardized training configuration, LoRA, AdaLoRA, and IA³ all predicted the positive class for 100% of the validation examples, producing an accuracy score of exactly 68.38%.

This score represents majority-class baseline collapse, not learned paraphrase comprehension. In contrast, Prefix Tuning reached 73.12% and Full Fine-Tuning reached 86.76%.
Figure 5: Confusion matrix diagnostics for BERT-base across SST-2, MRPC, and RTE, exposing the majority-class failure mode on MRPC.

AdaLoRA Training Dynamics on SST-2

AdaLoRA achieved an unexpectedly low 66.36 ± 1.84% accuracy on BERT/SST-2 despite its training loss steadily decreasing from 1.90 to 0.61 across epochs.

Inspection of predictions revealed strong class imbalance in its output probabilities (predicting the positive class with disproportionate confidence). This provides an important empirical lesson: consistent loss reduction does not guarantee balanced decision boundary convergence.

Figure 6: Smoothed cross-entropy training loss trajectories on SST-2 (rolling mean, window=7) across epochs with ±1σ ribbons.

Prefix Tuning Architectural Compatibility

Nine targeted runs (DistilBERT + Prefix Tuning across SST-2, MRPC, RTE for seeds 42–44) were skipped and recorded as N/A due to an architectural constraint: DistilBERT does not expose past-key-value cache arguments required by Prefix Tuning in the tested Transformers release.

07 / Evidence-Based Decision Guide

Practitioner Recommendations & Limitations

Choosing an adaptation method depends on hardware constraints, storage limits, and task characteristics.

Priority / Constraint Observed in Benchmark Practical Recommendation
Highest accuracy in this benchmark Full Fine-Tuning achieved highest accuracy (92.4% SST-2, 86.8% MRPC, 65.7% RTE) as the reference baseline. Use Full Fine-Tuning when GPU VRAM and checkpoint storage are unconstrained.
Best accuracy/resource trade-off LoRA achieved 89.56% on BERT/SST-2 and 87.35% on DistilBERT with ~35%–56% VRAM reduction. LoRA provides the most reliable general-purpose balance between resource savings and accuracy.
High accuracy with fixed VRAM Prefix Tuning reached 90.25% on BERT/SST-2 and resisted MRPC collapse better (73.12%). Consider Prefix Tuning for encoder backbones when higher parameter capacity (~11.9%) is acceptable.
Highest parameter efficiency IA³ tuned only 76,034 params (~0.07%) with a 6.3 MB checkpoint, but accuracy dropped to 80.39% on SST-2. Use IA³ when checkpoint size is the primary constraint and moderate accuracy loss is acceptable.
Small / Imbalanced Datasets Low-parameter adapters collapsed to predicting the majority class on MRPC (68.38%) under fixed learning rates. Always validate PEFT decision boundaries on small datasets rather than assuming hyperparameter transferability.
Statistical Caution & Inferential Limits
Comparisons are based on three random seeds (`42`, `43`, `44`), yielding n = 3 paired observations (df = 2). Paired t-tests and confidence intervals are reported as exploratory diagnostics rather than confirmatory proofs of population-level significance. Primary evidence should be judged on effect sizes, mean ± standard deviation, and measured computational savings.
Figure 7: Individual evaluation accuracy across random seeds (Seeds 42, 43, 44) alongside seed means.

Benchmark Validity Boundaries

  • Fixed Hyperparameters All algorithms were tested under fixed learning rates (2 × 10-5 / 1 × 10-3). Methods may perform better under independent per-task hyperparameter tuning.
  • Task Scope Evaluated on three GLUE classification benchmarks. Results may differ on generation, reasoning, or long-context tasks.
  • Hardware Specifics Measurements collected on an NVIDIA RTX 3050 Laptop GPU (6 GB VRAM). Memory fragmentation and timings vary on data-center GPUs.
